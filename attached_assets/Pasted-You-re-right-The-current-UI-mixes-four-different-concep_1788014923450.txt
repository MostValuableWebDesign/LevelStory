You’re right. The current UI mixes four different concepts:

- **Strategies:** your actual trade methods.
- **Setup components/actions:** pullback, consolidation, patience candle confirmation.
- **Outcomes:** qualified trade, stop, target, runner exit.
- **Diagnostics:** rejected setup, weak ORB probe, ambiguity.

Also, “available” should mean a strategy has passed defined fitness requirements—not merely that one historical example was found.

The following are my actual strategies:

1. Patience Candle Continuation
2. Strong Breakout After Consolidation
3. ORB Break, Pullback, and Continuation

Do not label actions, setup components, outcomes, or diagnostics as strategies.

A. DEFINE THE FOUR STRATEGIES

1. PATIENCE_CANDLE_CONTINUATION

A continuation setup requiring:

- Confirmed directional trend.
- Pullback or consolidation at a qualifying level.
- A completed patience candle.
- Entry only on the immediate next five-minute candle.
- Long entry 3–4 ticks above the patience-candle high.
- Short entry 3–4 ticks below the patience-candle low.
- If the immediate-next candle does not reach the entry price, the setup expires.

2. STRONG_BREAKOUT_AFTER_CONSOLIDATION

Requires:

- Approximately 45–60 minutes of consolidation.
- Defined consolidation high and low.
- Strong breakout beyond the consolidation boundary.
- Supporting breakout volume.
- No entry on a slight low-volume probe.
- If the breakout is weak, wait for a stronger push.
- Apply the required patience/pullback entry rules when the formula requires them.

3. ORB_BREAK_PULLBACK_CONTINUATION

Requires:

- ORB/NTZ established from the first three completed five-minute candles, 9:30–9:45 a.m. ET.
- Meaningful break beyond ORB high or low.
- Direction agrees with the 15-minute trend.
- Strong continuation and supporting volume.
- Pullback to ORB/NTZ or another qualifying critical level.
- Valid patience candle.
- Entry only on the immediate-next candle at the 3–4 tick buffer.

ORB and NTZ use the same high and low boundaries.

4. EQUIVALENT_CANDLE_REVERSAL

Requires:

- Equivalent opposing full-body candles near a major level.
- Similar candle body sizes within an explicit deterministic tolerance.
- Small or absent trend-side wicks as defined by the formula.
- Reversal context at a major support/resistance, prior-session, ORB, VWAP, or EMA level.
- Volume confirmation when required.
- A deterministic entry and invalidation rule.
- Do not classify equivalent candles away from a meaningful level as a valid reversal strategy.

D. STRATEGY READINESS

Strategies should become available as they become fit.

Introduce these statuses:

1. NOT_ENOUGH_EVIDENCE
   The strategy exists but lacks sufficient historical examples.

2. COLLECTING_EVIDENCE
   Examples are being reviewed, but the minimum qualification sample has not been reached.

3. READY_FOR_VALIDATION
   Minimum evidence exists and a deterministic historical validation can run.

4. VALIDATION_FAILED
   The strategy failed one or more fitness requirements.

5. SHADOW_CANDIDATE
   The strategy passed initial validation and may be observed in Shadow Mode.

6. FIT_AVAILABLE
   The strategy passed all required readiness gates and is available for Shadow Mode decisions.

7. PAUSED
   The strategy was manually paused or failed later monitoring.

Use “available” only for FIT_AVAILABLE strategies.

A strategy with one historical example is not automatically available.

Make readiness criteria configurable and visible.

At minimum require:

- Deterministic rule definition is complete.
- No unresolved rule ambiguity.
- Minimum number of valid historical setups.
- Minimum number of completed trades.

- Acceptable ambiguity rate.

Do not secretly choose financial thresholds.

Create explicit configurable fields for:

- Minimum completed-trade count.
- Minimum holdout count.
- Minimum expectancy.
- Maximum drawdown.

One nuance: the **patience candle is both a strategy and a shared entry mechanism**. It can exist as “Patience Candle Continuation,” while ORB and breakout strategies can also reuse the patience-candle entry rule. LevelStory should record that shared dependency without merging all four strategies together.
