You’re right. A strict “one trade per arm” rule could remove valid second-chance entries. The issue is not re-entry itself—it’s unlimited re-entry without evidence that conditions improved.

Use a controlled re-entry rule:

- Permit up to **two entries per breakout/pullback arm**.
- After the first stop, require a **new patience candle**; never reuse the first P candle.
- The immediate next candle must confirm the new patience candle by the full **8-tick entry buffer**.
- The new P→E sequence must remain outside the NTZ.
- The new patience candle must still be within the **12-tick pullback range** of a qualifying key level.
- The original breakout direction must remain structurally valid.
- Reject re-entry if price has closed back inside the NTZ or broken the underlying setup’s invalidation level.
- Calculate the second trade’s entry, stop, and target entirely from its own P→E sequence. Never reuse the first trade’s stop.
- After two losses on the same arm, retire that arm until a genuinely new breakout structure forms.
- Grade the second attempt lower unless it has stronger confluence than the first.

A particularly useful distinction is:

- **Early entry:** the first trade stopped, but the breakout structure and qualifying level still held. A new P→E can authorize another attempt.
- **Failed thesis:** price invalidated the breakout, returned through the NTZ, or broke the structural level. No re-entry from that arm.

I would also avoid automatically reducing the target on the second entry. First fix repeated low-quality entries, then compare 1R performance separately.
