Build a full-stack trading discipline application called “LevelStory.”

PURPOSE

LevelStory should help remove emotion from my trading by enforcing my trading formula as a strict, step-by-step decision system.

This first version must operate in SHADOW MODE only. It may identify setups, generate alerts, calculate position size, and record hypothetical trades, but it must never place real orders.

The app should treat every trade as “NO TRADE” until all required conditions have been confirmed.

CORE TRADING FORMULA

1. MARK IMPORTANT LEVELS

Automatically calculate and display:

- Premarket high
- Premarket low
- Yesterday’s high
- Yesterday’s low
- Day-before-yesterday high
- Day-before-yesterday low
- First 15-minute opening-range high
- First 15-minute opening-range low
- VWAP
- 200-period moving average or EMA
- Relevant Fibonacci retracement levels
- Nearby critical support and resistance levels

Every level should have a clear label and its price.

2. DETERMINE THE 15-MINUTE TREND

Use the 15-minute chart as the primary trend timeframe.

Classify the trend as:

- Bullish
- Bearish
- Neutral or unclear

Display the evidence used to determine the trend, including:

- Price relative to VWAP
- Price relative to the 200 MA/EMA
- Direction and slope of the 200 MA/EMA
- Recent market structure
- Higher highs and higher lows
- Lower highs and lower lows

Do not qualify a trade when the 15-minute trend is unclear unless it is explicitly being evaluated as a bonus reversal.

3. DEFINE THE NTZ

NTZ means Non-Trading Zone.

The default NTZ is the area between the high and low of the first completed 15-minute candle after the regular market opens.

The app must:

- Draw and shade the NTZ on the chart
- Display its high, low, and width
- Prevent normal trend entries while price remains inside the NTZ
- Require a completed candle to close outside the NTZ
- Clearly display “INSIDE NTZ — NO TRADE” or “OUTSIDE NTZ”

Allow the NTZ definition to be changed in Settings later.

4. DETECT AN OPENING-RANGE BREAK

A valid ORB setup requires:

- The first 15-minute opening range is complete
- Price breaks outside the opening range in the direction of the 15-minute trend
- A candle closes outside the opening range
- Price pulls back after the breakout
- The pullback reaches a critical level, VWAP, 200 MA/EMA, opening-range boundary, previous-session level, or Fibonacci retracement area

Do not allow chasing a breakout. A pullback is mandatory.

5. EVALUATE THE PULLBACK

A valid entry must pull back to at least one of the following:

- Opening-range high or low
- Premarket high or low
- Yesterday’s high or low
- Day-before-yesterday high or low
- VWAP
- 200 MA/EMA
- Critical support or resistance
- Fibonacci retracement area
- Another configured EMA

Display all confluences at the pullback.

Examples:

- ORB high + 38.2% Fibonacci retracement
- Yesterday’s high + VWAP
- Premarket low + 200 EMA

The app must always require a pullback to a critical level or EMA before qualifying a normal entry.

6. CHECK PULLBACK VOLUME

Compare pullback volume with:

- Breakout volume
- Average volume of recent candles
- Relative volume

A lower-volume pullback should support trend continuation.

A high-volume pullback against the trade direction must create a visible warning:

“HIGH-VOLUME PULLBACK — POSSIBLE REVERSAL”

Make the exact volume thresholds configurable. Do not silently invent permanent thresholds. Begin with clearly labeled default values that can be changed and tested.

7. CALCULATE FIBONACCI RETRACEMENT

For bullish setups, calculate Fibonacci retracement from the relevant swing low to swing high.

For bearish setups, calculate it from the relevant swing high to swing low.

Display:

- 23.6%
- 38.2%
- 50%
- 61.8%
- 78.6%

Highlight the configured preferred pullback zone.

Require the app to show which swing points were used. Allow me to correct those swing points manually.

Fibonacci should be treated as confluence, not as a standalone entry signal.

8. REQUIRE A PATIENCE CANDLE

I do not trade without seeing a patience candle.

A patience candle is mandatory for every normal entry.

My initial patience-candle definition is:

- Price has already pulled back to a critical level
- The next completed candle closes within the range of the previous candle
- Its close supports the direction of the 15-minute trend
- For a bullish setup, the patience candle should reject the level and close with bullish intent
- For a bearish setup, the patience candle should reject the level and close with bearish intent
- The candle must be completely closed before it can qualify

Show the previous candle and patience candle visually on the chart.

Display:

- “WAITING FOR PATIENCE CANDLE”
- “PATIENCE CANDLE FORMING”
- “VALID PATIENCE CANDLE”
- “INVALID PATIENCE CANDLE”

Never qualify an entry from an incomplete candle.

Make the exact patience-candle requirements configurable so this definition can be refined through testing.

9. LET LEVELS TELL THE STORY

At every important level, record how price behaves.

Classify the interaction as:

- Clean break
- Rejection
- Hold
- Failed breakout
- Retest
- Consolidation
- High-volume reversal
- Doji or indecision
- Equivalent opposing candles

Show a chronological “Level Story” feed, for example:

- 9:45 AM: Price closed above ORB high
- 10:00 AM: Low-volume pullback held ORB high
- 10:15 AM: Bullish patience candle confirmed
- 10:30 AM: Yesterday’s high rejected price

Do not merely display levels. Explain what price did when it reached them.

10. ENTRY QUALIFICATION

A normal setup can qualify only when:

- Important levels are marked
- The 15-minute trend is identified
- Price is outside the NTZ
- A completed candle broke the opening range
- A pullback occurred
- The pullback reached a critical level, VWAP, 200 MA/EMA, Fibonacci area, or configured EMA
- Pullback volume passed the safety check
- A valid patience candle completely closed
- Risk and position size are acceptable
- Daily safety limits have not been reached

Display one of these decisions:

- NO TRADE
- WAITING
- SETUP FORMING
- SETUP QUALIFIED
- POSSIBLE REVERSAL
- RISK LOCKOUT

For every decision, explain exactly which rules passed and which rules failed.

11. STOP MANAGEMENT

Separate two types of stops:

A. Catastrophe stop

- A hard, non-negotiable protective stop
- Protects against gaps, halts, extreme volatility, or system failure
- Must always exist in paper or future live trading

B. Strategy or thesis stop

- Based on the invalidation of the setup
- May require a completed candle to close through the relevant level

For a runner:

- Do not exit the runner only because of a small pullback
- Track the last level that should hold
- Wait for that level to break according to the configured runner rule
- Show exactly which level is protecting the runner
- Never allow runner logic to override the catastrophe stop

12. PROFIT MANAGEMENT

Do not require price to touch an exact target before taking profit because it may approach the level and reverse.

For each upcoming profit level:

- Display the exact level
- Create a configurable profit buffer before that level
- Suggest partial profit inside the buffer
- Allow the buffer to use cents, percentage, ticks, or ATR
- Track partial profit and runner size separately

Example:

If the next resistance level is $190.00 and the configured buffer is $0.05, the planned partial-profit price should be $189.95.

13. BONUS REVERSAL SETUPS

Reversals are optional bonus setups and must be kept separate from normal trend-following setups.

Watch for:

- A doji candle at a key level
- Equivalent red and green candles
- Strong rejection at a critical level
- Failed breakout
- High-volume pullback
- Momentum divergence when available
- A break of the reversal structure

Define “equivalent red and green candles” initially as two nearby opposing candles whose body sizes are within a configurable percentage of each other.

A doji or equivalent-candle pattern is only an alert. It must not qualify an entry by itself.

Require level context, confirmation, risk validation, and a patience candle before qualifying a reversal.

14. RISK CONTROLS

Include configurable controls for:

- Account size
- Maximum risk per trade
- Maximum daily loss
- Maximum trades per day
- Maximum position size
- Maximum spread
- Minimum liquidity
- No averaging down
- No duplicate orders
- Stale-data detection
- Emergency kill switch

Calculate position size using:

Maximum risk in dollars ÷ absolute difference between entry and catastrophe stop

If the calculated position violates a safety rule, block the setup.

15. SHADOW TRADE JOURNAL

Record every evaluated setup, including setups that were rejected.

Store:

- Symbol
- Date and time
- Long or short
- 15-minute trend
- All marked levels
- NTZ status
- ORB status
- Pullback level
- Fibonacci confluence
- VWAP and 200 MA/EMA status
- Volume analysis
- Patience-candle status
- Entry, stop, target, and position size
- Rules passed
- Rules failed
- Screenshots or chart snapshots when available
- Hypothetical result
- Maximum favorable excursion
- Maximum adverse excursion
- Reason for exit
- Notes

The journal should help determine which parts of the formula improve or hurt performance.

USER INTERFACE

Create a professional desktop-first trading interface with:

- A large primary chart
- Clearly labeled levels
- Shaded NTZ
- 15-minute trend status
- Current decision panel
- Required-rule checklist
- Level Story timeline
- Volume warning panel
- Fibonacci controls
- Entry, stop, target, and position-size planner
- Runner management panel
- Shadow-trade journal
- Strategy Settings page

Use restrained colors:

- Green for bullish confirmation
- Red for bearish confirmation or danger
- Amber for waiting and warnings
- Gray for neutral information

Avoid a generic admin-dashboard appearance. The chart, decision, checklist, and risk controls should dominate the interface.

Create responsive mobile layouts, but optimize the main experience for desktop trading.

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

- Use simulated market data initially
- Keep all broker-order functions disabled
- Clearly label the application “SHADOW MODE”
- Separate market data, strategy rules, risk management, and UI into different modules
- Store every configurable rule in a central strategy configuration
- Do not bury trading thresholds inside UI components
- Include loading, empty, stale-data, disconnected, error, and market-closed states
- Add automated tests for every required trading rule
- Use only completed candles for signals requiring candle confirmation
- Prevent look-ahead bias in replay and backtesting
- Include realistic slippage, spread, and fees in simulated results
- Record why every signal was accepted or rejected
- Do not add a live broker connection until paper-trading results have been reviewed

IMPORTANT

Do not claim this strategy is profitable.

Do not invent missing trading rules without labeling them as assumptions.

Whenever a rule is subjective, make it configurable and display the current definition in plain language.

Before building, create a structured implementation plan and list every trading term that still needs an exact numerical definition.
