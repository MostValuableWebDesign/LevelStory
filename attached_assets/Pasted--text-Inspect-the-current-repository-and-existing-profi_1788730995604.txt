Inspect the current repository and existing profit-target implementation before editing.

Update or complete my profit-taking strategy so it searches through multiple causal major levels and indicators instead of automatically using the nearest level.

Purpose:

- Take profit near a meaningful major level or indicator when it offers at least 1R.
- Skip target levels that are too close to the entry.
- Continue searching outward for the next eligible primary level.
- Use only levels visible in the completed entry snapshot.
- Keep the target within a realistically achievable range.
- Fall back to exactly 1R when no eligible level exists.
- Distinguish between a harmless nearby indicator and a major structural obstacle that makes 1R unrealistic.

Create the target-level snapshot only after the immediately adjacent entry-confirmation candle E has completed.

- Entry price
- Strategy stop
- Risk in points and ticks
- Direction

- Premarket high and low
- Previous-session high and low

- VWAP

- Other explicitly governed primary levels
- Level source timestamp
- Formula version and hash
- Source fingerprint

2. Calculate 1R from the modeled entry and stop

Use:

`RiskPoints = abs(EntryPrice - StrategyStopPrice)`

`OneRPriceLong = EntryPrice + RiskPoints`

`OneRPriceShort = EntryPrice - RiskPoints`

This is structural trade risk based on the modeled fill and authoritative stop. Do not base it on account balance.

Reject invalid plans when:

- Entry or stop is unavailable.
- Risk is zero or negative.
- Prices are non-finite.
- The stop is on the wrong side of the entry.

3. Build the directional target list

For a long trade, include only levels above the entry.

For a short trade, include only levels below the entry.

Eligible target sources should include:

Primary structural levels:

- Major resistance for long trades
- Major support for short trades
- ORB/NTZ boundary
- Premarket high or low
- Previous-session high or low
- Two-sessions-ago high or low

- VWAP

4. Determine the executable price before evaluating R

`TargetBufferTicks = clamp(ceil(ATR14Ticks × 0.05), 1, 2)`

- Place the executable target below that near side by the target buffer.

For short trades:

- Use the near side of a support zone.

Long:
`ExecutableTarget = IndicatorPrice - TargetBuffer`

Short:
`ExecutableTarget = IndicatorPrice + TargetBuffer`

Evaluate reward-to-risk using the executable buffered target—not the raw level.

5. Skip targets that are too close

A level is too close when its buffered executable target provides less than 1R:

`TargetR = abs(ExecutableTarget - EntryPrice) / RiskPoints`

If:

`TargetR < 1.00`

do not select that level as the profit target.

Record:

`TARGET_LEVEL_SKIPPED_BELOW_1R`

Then continue searching outward in the trade direction for the next eligible primary level or indicator.

Do not immediately fall back to 1R merely because the nearest level was too close. Search every eligible level in the frozen snapshot first.

6. Define the achievable search range

A level is within the achievable search range when its executable target is:

- At least 1.00R from entry
- No more than 1.50R from entry
- No more than 20 MES ticks from entry

Use:

`MaximumTargetDistance = min(1.50 × RiskPoints, 20 × TickSize)`

A level beyond that distance is not eligible for selection.

Record:

`TARGET_LEVEL_SKIPPED_BEYOND_ACHIEVABLE_RANGE`

The 20-tick level-search limit applies to selecting key levels. It does not shorten the 1R fallback target when the structural risk itself is wider than 20 ticks.

7. Search order

Sort eligible levels by directional distance from the entry, nearest to farthest.

Evaluate each one in order:

1. Confirm it is profitable and directionally valid.
2. Calculate its buffered executable target.
3. Calculate its TargetR.
4. Skip it if it provides less than 1R.
5. Skip it if it is beyond the achievable range.
6. Select the first level that passes every rule.

When multiple levels occupy the same confluence cluster, use this precedence for the representative target:

1. Major support/resistance zone
2. ORB/NTZ boundary
3. Premarket high/low
4. Previous-session high/low
5. Two-sessions-ago high/low
6. 200 MA
7. VWAP
8. Other eligible indicators

Preserve the other clustered levels as supporting confluence.

8. Handle obstructing levels sensibly

Do not blindly target a farther level when a major structural barrier blocks the path to 1R.

Differentiate between:

A. Nearby diagnostic or soft indicator

Examples:

- VWAP
- 200 MA
- A nonstructural indicator level

If it is below 1R, skip it and continue searching for the next eligible target.

B. Hard structural obstacle

Examples:

- Confirmed major resistance directly above a long entry
- Confirmed major support directly below a short entry
- A governed major zone that price must cross before reaching 1R

If a hard structural obstacle exists before 1R and the strategy does not permit targeting beyond it, reject the trade with:

`INSUFFICIENT_REWARD_TO_RISK`

Do not pretend a farther target is achievable through an obstructing major level.

Record whether every skipped level was:

- Too close
- Beyond the achievable range
- Wrong direction
- Duplicate/confluence
- Diagnostic only
- A hard structural obstruction

9. Target selection and fallback

Use this decision order:

A. Eligible key level found

Select the first buffered target satisfying:

`1.00R <= TargetR <= 1.50R`

and within 20 MES ticks.

B. No eligible key level found and no hard obstruction exists

Use exactly 1R:

Long:
`Target = EntryPrice + RiskPoints`

Short:
`Target = EntryPrice - RiskPoints`

Record:

`ONE_R_FALLBACK_NO_ELIGIBLE_LEVEL`

C. Hard structural obstacle exists before 1R

Reject with:

`INSUFFICIENT_REWARD_TO_RISK`

Do not enter the trade and do not include it in realized P/L.
